How Many Times Should You Repost the Same Video?

Most founders treat a finished video like a firework — light it once, watch it fade. We've learned the opposite is true. Here's how often you can reuse the same clip before you hit real diminishing returns.

The question we get more than almost any other: how many times can I repost the same video before people get annoyed? Founders ask it because they're terrified of looking repetitive, so they post a good clip once, watch it do fine, and move on. Then they wonder why their content feels like a treadmill — always new, never compounding.

Here's the honest answer. You are not posting enough. The repost ceiling is much higher than you think, and the real cost of under-reposting is that your best material dies before 90% of your audience ever sees it.

Why posting once is the actual mistake

Organic reach on a single post touches a sliver of your followers. On Instagram, a Reel might reach 10-30% of your audience on a good day. On LinkedIn, the algorithm serves your post to a test batch and expands only if that batch engages in the first hour. On short-form broadly, most of your people are simply not online in the window your post lives.

So when you post a strong clip once and never again, you're not respecting your audience's intelligence — you're just leaving reach on the table. The people who saw it are a small, half-random sample. Everyone else never knew it existed.

We run the numbers on this constantly with clients. One recording becomes thirty-plus pieces, and the smart ones get posted more than once. If a clip earns its keep the first time, that's a signal to run it back — not retire it.

The reposting rules we actually use

Reposting isn't one tactic. It's three, and they have different ceilings.

Same clip, same platform

You can repost a genuinely strong short-form video to the same platform roughly every 30-45 days, and most people won't notice. We've seen a Reel do better on its second run than its first — different time of day, different slice of the algorithm's audience. The honest limit: don't run the same clip more than 3-4 times a year on one platform, and only if it performed above average the first time. Mediocre clips don't earn a rerun.

Same clip, different platform

This one has almost no ceiling, and it's where founders leave the most value. A clip that lives on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube Shorts, and your email is one piece of work reaching five distinct audiences with near-zero overlap. Posting the same video across platforms in the same week isn't lazy — it's basic distribution. Nobody is following you on all five and keeping a tally.

Same idea, new cut

The highest ceiling of all. Take the point that worked and film it again with a different hook, a different first three seconds, a tighter edit. This isn't a repost — it's a remix, and you can run a winning idea five different ways across a quarter. If a take lands, the message is working. Give it more shots at goal, not fewer.

The clips you're most sick of are usually the ones your audience has barely seen.

How to track reposts without a spreadsheet nightmare

The reason founders don't repost is operational, not strategic. They can't remember what they posted or how it did, so repeating feels risky. Fix the system and the behavior follows.

We keep a simple content library — every clip gets a row with the hook, the platforms it's run on, the dates, and a one-word performance note: strong, fine, or dead. That's it. When a busy week hits and nothing new is ready, you pull a strong clip that hasn't run in 30 days and you post it. No panic, no scrambling.

This is the same discipline behind a content system that survives your busy weeks. The buffer isn't just new footage — it's a backlog of proven pieces you're allowed to reuse. A clip that performed in March is an asset in June, not a liability.

Where reposting actually goes wrong

There are real limits, and pretending otherwise gets you burned.

Don't repost anything time-stamped — a live announcement, a seasonal promo, a reference to something that's since changed. Don't repost a dead clip hoping the second run saves it; it won't, and you'll teach yourself reposting doesn't work. Don't post the identical clip to the same platform two weeks apart — that's the window where the same humans scroll past it twice and it reads as filler.

And don't repost without checking the caption and hook still make sense. A 30-second recut with a fresh first line almost always outperforms a straight repost, because the hook is what the algorithm tests first anyway.

The through-line with every client we work with is the same: good content is expensive to make and cheap to distribute. Most founders have that backwards. They pour energy into one-time posts and treat distribution as free, when distribution is where the leverage actually lives. We built our one-studio-day-a-month model around exactly this — you create rarely, deliberately, and in volume, then you distribute relentlessly.

So the next time you finish a clip you're proud of, don't ask whether you can post it again. Ask how many ways you haven't posted it yet. That's the number that grows your audience.
